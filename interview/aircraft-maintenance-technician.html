Sample Interview Questions

Q1
Tell me about your airframe and powerplant experience — which aircraft types have you worked on?
Tip: State your A&P status plainly and be specific about airframes and engines (737, A320, CRJ, GA piston), heavy checks versus line maintenance, and your role on the crew.
Q2
Describe your experience with FAA documentation — logbook entries, sign-offs, and return to service.
Tip: Accurate entries per 14 CFR 43.9 and 43.11, work order sign-offs, MEL deferrals, and an emphatic stance against pencil-whipping.
Q3
How do you troubleshoot an intermittent avionics or electrical fault?
Tip: Wiring diagrams and system schematics, methodical isolation versus parts-swapping, voltage and continuity checks, service bulletins, and documenting findings.
Q4
Tell me about a time you found something wrong that someone else had already signed off on.
Tip: Speak-up culture, refusing to rubber-stamp, how you reported it, and awareness of voluntary reporting programs like ASRS.

Education & Certifications

Degrees

  • AOS degree
  • Certificate program (12-24 months)
  • Technical diploma

Certifications

  • FAA Airframe & Powerplant (A&P) certificate
  • FCC General Radiotelephone Operator License (GROL) — for avionics work
  • Inspection Authorization (IA) — for experienced mechanics

Career Paths

  • FAA Part 147 aviation maintenance technician school (18-24 months) plus the FAA written, oral, and practical exams
  • Military aircraft maintenance experience (for example Air Force crew chief) converted through FAA testing pathways
  • Entry helper or MRO apprenticeship roles while completing Part 147 training
